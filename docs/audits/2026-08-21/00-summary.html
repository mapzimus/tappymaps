Every finding below was reproduced in a browser. Nothing here is static analysis dressed up as a bug.

The through-line: this app fails silently. Across eight domains and hundreds of probe runs, the console was almost always clean. Sharing was broken for every data map — no error. The signed-in export tier had never worked — the user was told to check their connection. Undo destroyed county maps — no warning. Saves reported success and stored nothing.

That is why these survived so long. The app doesn't tell you when it breaks, and it doesn't tell the user either.

How this was found

Before any auditing, the project's own safety net turned out to be the first bug. npm run smoke reported "all 10 routes booted clean" on a page where zero states had rendered and every third-party library had failed to load — a broad net::ERR in its ignore list swallowed total CDN failure.

WHAT SMOKE SAW  : mode=design/make          -> reported "ok"
WHAT WAS REAL   : statePaths: 0, chroma/topojson/domtoimage/supabase all undefined
errors after its IGNORE list : 0   <- why it reported green

That was rewritten first, into 24 functional assertions, and then adversarially tested against injected regressions. It now runs in CI on every push.

Three findings worth reading in full

The GeoDraft difficulty ladder doesn't exist. Difficulty sets who picks first, not how well the AI plays — and pick order decides the round. Across 4,800 simulated matches, validated against real play:

PlayerEasyNormalHard
Perfect100.0%100.0%84.0%
Good99.0%87.3%42.0%
Casual78.5%37.0%5.0%
Random2.8%0.5%0.0%

Three settings, two of them identical for anyone who knows US geography, and one a cliff.

The payload is smaller than it looks. 744KB raw, but 174KB gzipped — the size panic is unfounded. The real waste is that 677KB of 971KB of parsed JavaScript never executes, three CDN scripts block <head> with no defer, and no-store on / re-downloads everything on every visit. Desktop boot is genuinely fast (FCP 144ms, zero long tasks, no memory leak across 140 navigations); slow 3G with 4× CPU throttling is 7.7s to usable.

The static game data is correct. All ten bundled GeoDraft tables were checked against ground truth across all 50 states — electoral votes sum to 535, presidential birthplaces to 45. Worth stating plainly, because it's the part a rewrite would most easily lose.

The rebuild

You asked for a genuine from-scratch rewrite, with my objection on the table. It's underway — and the first move was to make sure it can't lose anything.

All 23 data tables were lifted out of index.html mechanically, never retyped, and verified byte-identical against the running app:

FIPS_TO_STATE       51   DATA_MAP_DATASETS   39   TEMPLATES           28
THEME_LIST          16   COLOR_RAMPS         10   DRAFT_AREA          50
DRAFT_STATEHOOD     50   DRAFT_EV            50   DRAFT_BORDERS       50
...23 tables total — all byte-identical to the live app.

On top of that, the modules that fix a whole class of bug rather than one instance:

  • One map renderer. The old app grew five near-duplicate SVG builders — editor, Arcade, two GeoDraft boards, Hub, plus a sixth server-side — which is why an export and an OG image could disagree about the same map.
  • A total URL-state codec. 18 adversarial tests. Rejects __proto__ without polluting the prototype, survives every hostile input tried, tolerates the URL-safe base64 and stripped padding that chat apps inflict on share links, and still decodes every legacy link.
  • Safe storage. Every read shape-guarded, every write reporting whether it landed — the thing that made a corrupt key brick the old app and a full quota lose a save in silence.
  • DOM built escaped by construction, so the innerHTML XSS class has nowhere to live.

My honest read, restated once. The value in those 15,809 lines was never the code — it's the knowledge encoded in it: export capture fidelity, legend anchoring against the SVG content rect rather than the container, the three-layer Chrome autofill defeat, 39 audited ACS variable codes.

A rewrite that re-derives those meets them again as fresh bugs. So the approach is to carry the knowledge forward deliberately — extract and verify the data, rebuild the structure — rather than starting from an empty file and hoping. That is what "from the ground up, knowing what we know now" should mean.

What needs you

  • The database. The client queries user_maps, map_reports, gallery_publish_counts and classroom_codes. None exist in the live project — the migrations were written and never run. Cloud sync, the public gallery, and the $12/mo Classroom tier have therefore never worked. You said redo it; the new schema is being designed rather than the old migrations replayed.
  • Taking money. There is no cancellation path, no billing portal, and no Terms, Privacy or refund policy anywhere — while the UI says "Cancel anytime". The Stripe billing portal endpoint is on the list. The policies are yours or a lawyer's.
  • Two P0s are product decisions, not bugs. Client-side entitlements and unwatermarked anonymous exports can't be fully closed in a client-rendered app. What can be server-enforced is the quota, the Census proxy, and anything behind Supabase RLS. Worth deciding how much leakage is acceptable before engineering against it.
